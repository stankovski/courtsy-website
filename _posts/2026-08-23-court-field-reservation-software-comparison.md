Choosing reservation software is mostly a question of fit. A neighborhood with one shared tennis court has different needs from a commercial racquet club or a parks department running programs, permits, and payments.

This guide compares options for HOA boards, apartment communities, property managers, and community recreation teams. It focuses on the booking workflow, administration scope, and published pricing rather than declaring a single winner.

## What community facilities usually need

The calendar itself is only part of the decision. Before comparing product checklists, decide how the facility will actually be used:

- Can people check availability from a phone before they make the trip?
- Are the booking rules understandable and appropriate for the community?
- Can managers block time for maintenance, events, or closures?
- Does the public view reveal only the information people need to coordinate use?
- Does the organization need wider operations such as memberships, programs, retail, permits, payments, or formal reporting?

The last question often separates a focused scheduling product from a full club or recreation-management suite.

## Courtsy: a focused community booking workflow

[Courtsy]({{ '/' | relative_url }}) is designed for shared courts, fields, and community spaces. A person opens a permanent link or scans a QR code, views the schedule, and uses passwordless email confirmation to reserve an available time. Public schedules show a booker&apos;s first name and last initial rather than an email address. See the [FAQ]({{ '/faq/' | relative_url }}) and [Privacy Policy]({{ '/privacy/' | relative_url }}) for the details.

The free plan includes a shareable court link and QR code, a daily mobile schedule, 30-minute booking blocks, confirmation and cancellation links, abandoned-reservation flags, and consecutive-booking limits. The [Community plan]({{ '/pricing/' | relative_url }}) adds verified resident email, fair-use policies, maintenance and event blocks, custom block sizes, and up to two facilities with custom shortlinks for $9.99/month after a 30-day trial.

**Consider Courtsy when:** a community wants a lightweight, mobile-first way to publish a schedule and apply local booking rules without adopting a full club-management system.

**Look elsewhere when:** you need a pro shop, membership billing, league management, program registration, municipal permits, or broader payment and reporting operations.

## CourtReserve and Playbypoint: club operations

[CourtReserve](https://courtreserve.com/pricing) publishes an annual-billing Launch plan at $159/month for eight courts. Its plan comparison includes court booking and lessons, events and programming, membership management, public booking, payments, reporting, and higher-tier integrations. That scope can suit a tennis, pickleball, or padel club with memberships and programming.

[Playbypoint](https://www.playbypoint.com/pricing) lists Essential at $199/month on an annual contract. Its published plan includes reservation, program, and lesson bookings, a mobile app, basic reporting, and announcements; higher plans add merchandise, club accounts, and a custom domain. It is worth evaluating when bookings are connected to a commercial club&apos;s programs and revenue operations.

**Consider these products when:** club operations such as memberships, lessons, programs, payments, retail, or player engagement are core requirements.

## RecDesk and CivicPlus: broader recreation administration

[RecDesk](https://recdesk.com/pricing/) lists an Essential plan starting at $3,835 per year. Its published modules include program management, online registration, facility scheduling and reservations, membership management, financial reporting, and league scheduling. This is a materially broader administration suite than a simple court calendar.

[CivicPlus Recreation Management](https://www.civicplus.com/recreation-management-software) describes program and registration management, facility and event reservations, payment processing, and cost-recovery reporting for recreation teams. Its public page asks prospective customers to request a demo rather than showing a price.

**Consider these products when:** facilities are part of wider parks-and-recreation work involving programs, registrations, permits, payments, public-sector reporting, or multiple operational teams.

## AllBooked and Teamup: flexible scheduling alternatives

[AllBooked](https://www.skedda.com/pricing) is Skedda&apos;s booking offering for sports, studios, coworking, and community use. Its public pricing page lists embedded pricing and payments, membership management, integrations, rules and roles, and public or private booking, with sales-led pricing.

[Teamup](https://www.teamup.com/pricing/) is a shared-calendar product. Its plans list sub-calendars, user limits, event signup, recurring events, permissions, and no-double-booking. It can be a sensible fit when schedule visibility is the main requirement and the organization is comfortable handling community-specific booking policies outside the calendar.

**Consider these products when:** you need flexible, general resource scheduling rather than a court- or field-specific booking workflow.

## A practical way to choose

Start with the smallest workflow that solves the real problem. Test the public schedule on a phone, sketch the actual booking rules, and identify who needs to change them. Then compare the total operating fit, not just a starting price.

- Choose a focused community scheduler when clear availability and fair shared access are the goal.
- Choose club software when memberships, lessons, programs, or commerce drive the operation.
- Choose a recreation suite when facilities connect to registration, permits, payments, and public-sector reporting.
- Choose a flexible calendar when the team needs general resource visibility more than a purpose-built reservation flow.
